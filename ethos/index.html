Ethos Abandoned Cart.

They saw an Ethos ad, started a life insurance application, got deep into it, and stopped. That was 6 to 12 months ago. These leads sell. The first 30 seconds decide whether you get to sell them.

Black serif in a card: say it word for word.Gray text: for you. Don't read it to the client.
Who they are

Usually higher income, busy, and short on the pickup. They don't want fluff. Be direct and know your stuff.

What you have

Name, phone, email, date of birth, age, address. Nothing else. No product, amount, beneficiary or submit date.

The ad they clicked

Usually term: "$500K for about $12 a month, no exam, in minutes." That price is for a healthy 30-year-old on a 10-year term. Their real quote came in higher, or the amount lower, and they left. Could still be anything: term, whole life, final expense, IUL.

Your position

You're the advisor assigned, not Ethos. Ethos is one door. You have [60+] carriers and can usually beat the price or raise the amount.

Three rules that close these
  • Short lines, then shut up. On the pickup, never talk more than 15 seconds without asking something. Silence after a question is where they talk themselves in.
  • Sell the same call, not a callback. Every branch tries to keep them on the phone and get to Step 02. If you must book, it's today, two specific times. Never "when works for you?"
  • Agree, then ask. "That's fair." "I'd be annoyed too." "Totally get it." Then the next question. Arguing on an aged lead ends the call.

How old are they?

Under 50, think term. Over 65, think whole life. In between, keep asking.

How healthy are they?

Serious conditions or a lot of medications push to whole life at any age.

What can they afford?

A smaller permanent policy they keep beats a bigger term policy they cancel.

Term (really healthy)

Pitch it when: under 50, really healthy, and they want a big number for a set time. Income, kids, mortgage.

Minimal medications, minimal conditions. Anything serious or a long med list, go whole life.

Coverage: $50K up to $1M+, depending on age. Most coverage for the lowest premium.

How: full amount, 75%, 50%.

IUL (really healthy)

Pitch it when: 50 to 65 (or younger with money), really healthy, and they want coverage that lasts and builds cash. Hardest to qualify for, highest premium.

Minimal medications, minimal conditions. Strictest underwriting of the three.

Coverage: up to about $500K. Has to be funded more to work well.

How: three monthly amounts off the illustration.

Whole life (any health)

Pitch it when: 65 and up, or health issues at any age, or they want the funeral covered and money left behind, or term didn't fit the budget.

Coverage: $50K is the max you'll see.

How: Basic, Recommended, Maximum.

Note

Making good money changes the amount, not the product. Health still decides.

Important

Ethos ads sell 10-year term to healthy 30-year-olds. A 55-year-old on two medications was never getting that number anywhere. Don't promise to beat the ad. Promise the most coverage their age and health can actually get, and show it honestly.

A · The annuity pivot

When the money is the sale.

For the client who's already set, the one making good money, or the one whose health won't get much life insurance but who has money sitting in a 401k, IRA, CDs or savings. Pick how you got here, then tap what they say.

Annuity pivot
Keeps going Book today Close it out
Rules
  • Fixed and indexed annuities only. Variable needs a securities license.
  • Don't tell anyone to sell stocks or mutual funds. The account moves as a direct rollover or transfer, never a check to the client.
  • Suitability or best-interest paperwork in most states. Replacement forms if there's an existing annuity.
  • "Doesn't lose when the market drops" is about principal in a fixed or indexed annuity. Never promise a return. Never quote a rate on this call.
Training appendix

Rebuttals for aged Ethos carts.

Expect skepticism, foggy memory, and "didn't I already handle this." Match their tone, don't over-explain, and keep them on this call. Step 01 handles the pickup objections live; this list is the long form for training and for objections that come up mid-script.

Booking rule · same day, next day at worst
  • Every callback gets offered today first, with two specific times: "[time] or [time] today?" Never "when works for you?"
  • Only if today is truly impossible: first thing tomorrow, again two specific times.
  • Never book further out than tomorrow. Appointments are disappointments; the further out, the colder they get.
  • Text the confirmation while you're still on the phone. Call 10 minutes early if you can.
